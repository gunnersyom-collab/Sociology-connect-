🧭 Research Process

  1. Choose a research topic – Identify a clear and relevant social issue.
  2. Identify the research problem – Clearly explain the problem your study will investigate.
  3. Review literature – Read previous studies related to your topic.
  4. Develop research questions – Create questions your study will answer.
  5. Set research objectives – Define one general objective and specific objectives.
  6. Choose methodology – Select qualitative, quantitative or mixed methods.
  7. Collect data – Gather information ethically and systematically.
  8. Analyze data – Organize and interpret your findings.
  9. Write the report – Present findings, discussion and conclusion.
  10. Reference sources – Give proper credit to academic sources.

❓ Research FAQ

What is research?

Research is a systematic process of collecting, analyzing and interpreting information to understand a problem or answer a question.

Why is research important?

Research helps us understand social problems and provides evidence for better decisions and solutions.

What is a research problem?

A research problem is an issue or situation that requires systematic investigation.

What is a research objective?

A research objective explains what the researcher wants to achieve through the study.

What is research methodology?

Research methodology explains how the researcher will collect, analyze and interpret data.

What is a literature review?

A literature review examines previous studies and academic knowledge related to the research topic.

What is qualitative research?

Qualitative research explores people's experiences, meanings, opinions and social realities.

What is quantitative research?

Quantitative research uses numerical data and statistical methods to study relationships, patterns or differences.
